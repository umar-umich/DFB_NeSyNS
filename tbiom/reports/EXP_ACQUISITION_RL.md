## 2. What the RL policies learned

| arm | cost chosen per fold | clips/video | seed spread in AUROC | training time (all seeds, all folds) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PPO (k=1) | 0.02, 0.02, 0.005, 0.02, 0.02 | 0.68 | 0.0021 | 11 s |
| PPO (k=2) | 0.02, 0.02, 0.02, 0.02, 0.02 | 0.93 | 0.0028 | 16 s |
| REINFORCE (k=1) | 0.02, 0.02, 0.01, 0.02, 0.002 | 0.77 | 0.0021 | 10 s |

Total wall clock for the whole pilot, including the cost search: **1.5 minutes** on CPU, against a four-hour budget. 60 policy-gradient iterations per run, 3 seeds, 5 folds, 3 arms.

The cost per extra clip was selected on the **fitting folds only**, by fitting-fold AUROC of the greedy policy, over the grid [0.0, 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02]. It came out at the top of the grid (0.02) in 12 of the 15 fold-arm cells, i.e. the fitting folds prefer a policy that mostly declines to acquire.

**The policies learn to stop.** At budget 1 PPO uses 0.68 clips per video and REINFORCE 0.77; at budget 2 PPO uses 0.93 of a possible 2. That is why they beat every other learned row in the table: the supervised selector is forced to spend its budget, while an RL policy can decline. The closer these policies get to never acquiring, the closer their AUROC gets to the no-acquisition baseline — which is the ceiling here, not the floor.

**None of them beats not acquiring.** PPO at budget 1 is $-0.0050$ with the interval excluding zero, and REINFORCE is $-0.0066$, also excluding zero. PPO at budget 2 is $-0.0016$ with the interval spanning zero, i.e. statistically indistinguishable from doing nothing, which is the best outcome available to a policy in this environment.

## 4. The two provenance questions

**(a) What are the existing learned rows?** The one-step row is a **supervised, full-information action-value regressor**: a `GradientBoostingRegressor` fitted on the fitting fold against the log-loss gain of *every* candidate window, then applied by argmax. It is not a contextual bandit -- a bandit observes the reward of the arm it pulled and nothing else, whereas this model is trained on all four arms' outcomes. The two-step row is **not** sequential greedy: it takes the first and second highest predicted gains from the same model evaluated once in the initial state, with no re-planning after the first observation. The labels in the table above have been corrected accordingly.

**(b) Is the shuffled-score oracle gain $+0.0806$ or $+0.0788$?** Both, and neither is wrong. Both used the **`conf`** aggregation rule; they differ only in the random permutation drawn. Over 20 permutations per fold the null oracle scores 0.9859 $\pm$ 0.0014 (min 0.9830, max 0.9885) against a no-acquisition baseline of 0.9068, i.e. a gain of **+0.0791 $\pm$ 0.0014**. The published $+0.0788$ and the recomputed $+0.0806$ are two draws from that distribution. The figure to quote is the permutation mean, +0.0791.

For completeness, the aggregation rule behind every number in this document is `conf`, the vacuity-weighted within-branch mean, which is what `EXP_ACQUISITION_PILOT.md` reports in its section 1 table. The `mean` and `max` rules appear only in that report's section 0 comparison.

## 5. Verdict

The pilot ran to completion inside the budget and changes nothing. No policy class tried here -- supervised full-information, PPO at two budgets, or REINFORCE -- improves on not acquiring an extra clip. The best result available is to spend nothing, and the policies that come closest to the baseline are the ones that learn to stop. Combined with the null oracle's $+0.079$, which is larger than the true-label oracle's $+0.031$ and is by construction uninformative, the evidence says the apparent headroom in this environment is selection on labels among correlated scores rather than information a policy could use.
